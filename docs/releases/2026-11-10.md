# Candidate authoring release — 10 November 2026

The candidate corpus now contains 3,000 saved questions, exactly 1,000 for each of Step 1, Step 2 CK and Step 3, across 123 batch files. The manifest separately records 24 rejected and 13 unresolved candidates. Its 2,264 editorial-screened and 736 structural-only entries describe automated/provisional screening, not qualified medical approval. All saved candidates retain AI-authored and medically-unreviewed provenance.

This update preserves historical batch files and manifest content hashes against the batches 1–28 and 1–32 baselines. The pipeline includes batches 25–41 and preserves existing disk files and manifest decisions unless overwrite is explicitly requested. Citation handling recognizes punctuation and normalized www/hostname forms, preserves PMIDs in shortened citation labels, and includes regression tests. One new Step 3 reference label was shortened to satisfy the existing import schema, preserving its URL and PMID and updating its candidate hash.

All 3,000 saved candidate records passed the application content schema, stable-code uniqueness, manifest hash correspondence, five-option/single-answer checks, per-option explanation presence, reference presence and authorship-label checks. These checks do not establish answer correctness, source support or calibrated difficulty. The retrieval and claim-mapping ledgers preserve unsupported or partially supported claims for further review.

This is a source/application deployment. It does not import the new candidate corpus into production, grant it medical approval, or change the existing live bank's owner-authorized delivery permissions. Existing questions, history and learner accounts are preserved; no production migration, seed, reset or content overwrite is needed. Broader rights/dependency/ownership and qualified-review gaps remain unverified.

Raw reference-retrieval scratch files, one-off Python research helpers and browser debug artifacts remain local. The committed release includes the candidate batches, their TypeScript specifications, review/evidence ledgers, preservation baselines and reusable tooling changes.
